# Automotive Manufacturing Processes

> **Educational Reference Notice**: This document is general educational reference material for automotive benchmarking and engineering analysis. It does not represent proprietary or confidential data from any specific OEM or third-party teardown provider.

## 1. Sheet Metal Stamping (Pressing)
- **Process Overview**: Sheet metal coils are blanked and formed through high-tonnage mechanical or hydraulic press lines using progressive or transfer dies. Typical operations include deep drawing, piercing, trimming, and flanging.
- **Cycle Time & Economics**: Highly efficient cycle times (often 10 to 30 strokes per minute), making stamping the standard for high-volume automotive production. Initial tooling and die investments are very high (millions of dollars per stamping line), requiring high production volumes to amortize tooling costs.
- **Typical Components**: Body side outers, door inner and outer panels, floor pans, roof panels, and structural chassis brackets.

## 2. High-Pressure Die Casting (HPDC) and Megacasting
- **Process Overview**: Molten non-ferrous metal (primarily aluminum alloys like A380 or AlSi10MnMg, or magnesium alloys like AM60) is injected under high velocity and pressures exceeding 1,000 bar into hardened steel molds.
- **Advantages**: Produces complex thin-walled structural geometries, integrated mounting bosses, and variable cross-sectional thicknesses that cannot be made by sheet stamping. Facilitates part consolidation—merging 30 to 70 stamped steel parts into a single structural casting (e.g., rear underbody megacastings).
- **Tooling and Cycle Times**: Requires massive casting machines (6,000 to 9,000-ton clamping force) with cycle times ranging between 60 and 120 seconds.
- **Typical Components**: Shock towers, subframes, engine blocks, transmission cases, and large battery tray housings.

## 3. Plastic Injection Molding
- **Process Overview**: Granular polymer pellets (polypropylene, ABS, nylon, polycarbonate, or glass-filled thermoplastics) are melted and pushed through an injection screw into precision-machined steel tool cavities.
- **Characteristics**: Fast cycle times (20 to 60 seconds), high dimensional repeatability, low scrap rates, and excellent surface finishes (capable of Class-A gloss or molded-in grain texture).
- **Design Freedom**: Enables snap-fits, integral wiring channels, and multi-shot molding (combining soft-touch TPE over rigid plastic).
- **Typical Components**: Interior door trim panels, dashboard consoles, HVAC ducting, battery module covers, and bumper fascias.

## 4. Hot and Cold Forging
- **Process Overview**: Heated or cold solid metal billets are subjected to localized compressive forces from hydraulic presses or drop hammers using impression dies.
- **Material Properties**: Unlike casting, forging retains continuous directional metal grain flow matching component contours, resulting in superior tensile strength, fracture toughness, and fatigue endurance under dynamic loading.
- **Tooling & Cost**: High die wear and finishing costs (often requiring post-forge CNC machining for tight tolerances).
- **Typical Components**: Critical suspension control arms, steering knuckles, wheel hubs, crankshafts, connecting rods, and transmission input shafts.
